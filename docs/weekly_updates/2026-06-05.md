  5. **MI kickoff + NC access-posture re-verification (2026-06-02 → 2026-06-03).** Started Michigan, modeled on Wisconsin. Most of WI structure carries over — registered lobbyists/agents, the "Employed By" employer graph (MI's analog of WI's authorization edges), expenditure filings — and MI even has an itemized-spending-on-officials table WI lacked. **Caveat is a data-model fact, not a tooling gap:** Michigan's Lobby Registration Act is expenditure-centric, not bill-centric. Lobbyists report *how much* they spent but **not which bills they worked on**. The WI chain shape (client → lobbyist → bill → sponsor) has no MI equivalent; MI's MVP is a who-and-how-much dataset, not a bill-attribution one. **Decisions locked:** entity + expenditure MVP, 2025 vintage. **NC:** registration data is free bulk (we have ~3,000 lobbyist–client pairs + directories); activity/expenditure is gated behind a JS-only per-record search that prohibits scripted retrieval, or a paid Data Subscription Service. NC becomes a clean **legal-vs-practical** datapoint: public by statute (Chapter 120C), gated in practice. Constructive path: public-records request to the SoS for the bulk file, citing the public-inspection mandate.

  > **Access-posture principle (reused across states):** A state's online-search Terms of Use is a click-through contract of adhesion and does **not** override a statutory public-records obligation. When a state gates access to statutorily-public lobbying data, that barrier is a **practical-availability finding for our legal-vs-practical map — not a stop**. The statute-honoring lever is a public-records request; refusal or paywall is itself the documentable result. (Distinction kept clean: the statute typically guarantees *access*, often satisfiable by per-record inspection or fee-based copies — not specifically *bulk machine-readable* provision — so the request/demand is the instrument; scraping, legally defensible post-*hiQ v. LinkedIn* but with practical/optics risk, stays in reserve.)

## Why this week mattered

**Two states now have complete chains, structurally different.** WI (shipped 2026-05-27, ~115K rows) required IPF to model the lobbyist↔bill leg because WI lobbyists report only aggregate hours. NY (shipped 2026-06-08, ~84K rows) is a pure join because NY discloses the lobbyist→bill link directly. The contrast is the project's thesis in miniature: every state's disclosure regime imposes its own data shape, no universal pipeline survives contact with state #2, and *that* is itself a finding for the legal-vs-practical-availability map. The Anna Karenina principle (per-state pipelines are bespoke; shared infrastructure lives downstream of extraction) is now operating as a working assumption, not a hypothesis.

**Cross-state CPI 2015 at N=5 produced honest signal at 50% match rate.** That sounds bad on its face and it's worth being direct about it: it *would* be bad if CPI 2015 were the ground-truth for state-of-disclosure-law extraction. The failure-mode trends doc (six trends; 2026-06-06) reframes most of the gap as **projection-translation problems**, not extraction problems — the LLM is reading statutes faithfully, the projection function is over-constraining the mapping to CPI's specific tier definitions. Trends 1+6+2 are tractable as projection/engineering work; the rest are the v2.2 schema gap we already knew about from the 2026-05-21 Tier-2 finding ("model right, schema can't represent the answer"). The N=5 → N=10 extension is queued.
